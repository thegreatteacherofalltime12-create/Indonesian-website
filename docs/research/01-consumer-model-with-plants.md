## Start here: the 3-5 things that decide whether this works at all

1. **Who is the US legal person — and confirm in writing it is not me.** Every blocking issue below converges on one US-resident person or entity: the PPQ 587/621 plant permit holder (APHIS issues permits only to US residents), the importer of record with the CBP bond and resident agent (19 CFR 141.18), the Shopify Payments/Stripe account representative (US residential address, SSN/ITIN, equity disclosure), the CAN-SPAM postal address, the returns address, the Delaware registered agent. In a loose "I'll just build the site" arrangement that person becomes the developer by default, with legal liability for every seized plant box, every duty bill, and every Prop 65 or product-liability letter. Ask it first, get the answer in writing.
2. **Evidence, not assurance, that shipping to the US has been done before.** Ask for one prior US shipment's documents: phytosanitary certificate, V-Legal document / ETPIK number, DHL waybill or bill of lading, and the CBP entry showing who was importer of record. If none exist, "shipping is figured out" means FOB Semarang (the easy half), and the plant side does not yet exist as a US-legal operation.
3. **The plant species list may kill the plant business outright.** Ficus (all species), Dracaena, Hibiscus, Jasminum, Syzygium, bamboo and nearly all ornamental palms from Indonesia are NAPPRA — no permit can import them as live plants. All orchids, Nepenthes and cycads are CITES-listed. Everything must ship bare-root, no soil. If the range depends on these, there is no US plant catalog to build.
4. **The payment stack the developer assumed does not exist for an Indonesian entity.** Shopify Payments, Stripe (Preview/invite-only, and Indonesian accounts cannot process cross-border) and WooPayments are all unavailable. Xendit/Midtrans charge in IDR only and force the store currency to IDR. The realistic choices are: PayPal-only checkout in USD (~8-10% all-in), an IDR storefront for US shoppers, or a US LLC that then becomes the liable US person in item 1.
5. **Retail from Indonesia vs wholesale to the US is a different website.** Jepara exporters sell by the container with 30-50% deposits, 6-12+ week production and FOB terms; single-item B2C from Indonesia carries per-parcel duty (de minimis is gone), fixed courier brokerage/MPF/phytosanitary costs that dwarf the 10% duty, 16-22 week made-to-order timelines that collide with the FTC 30-day Mail Order Rule, and no free returns. Decide which one before building a cart.

Send its NIB, NPWP and the KBLI codes on the NIB. — Xendit will not onboard an individual, Midtrans will, PayPal/Airwallex need company documents, and export needs NIB/NPWP; this name goes on the checkout terms, invoices and policy pages.

Please confirm in writing that none of these are me. — This is the liability question; the site's legal pages, checkout text and tax settings all name this party.

If PayPal is the main rail, do you accept roughly 4.4% + US$0.30 per transaction + 3% currency conversion + Shopify's 0.6-2% third-party fee, and do you already have a verified Indonesian PayPal Business account that has received US payments? — Sets the margin assumption baked into prices and whether checkout is PayPal-wallet-only.

Who owns it, who is the US-resident account representative, and who files its US taxes? — Only route to Shopify Payments, Shop Pay and real multi-currency; it also makes that LLC the importer/permit holder and creates US tax exposure (possible effectively-connected income). Is this retail to households or wholesale to businesses, and what average order value and annual US sales by state do you expect? — Decides sales-tax nexus tracking (US$100k/200-order thresholds in most states; any US-held inventory creates immediate physical nexus) and whether checkout is card/PayPal or invoice/wire.

Has an accountant reviewed US income-tax exposure for a foreign seller (ECI vs the US-Indonesia treaty), and Indonesia's export-proceeds retention rule (only bites at ≥US$250k per declaration in listed sectors; whether live plants are a listed HS code is unresolved)? — May force a US entity or route large orders to an offline invoice flow.

How am I paid, from which entity and country, and do I have a written contract stating I am a contractor and not the seller, importer, permit holder or guarantor, with indemnity? — Protects the developer; also determines who owns the store, domain, accounts and product data if the partnership ends.

### Plants — US import compliance

12. Send the exact list of plant taxa you intend to sell (scientific genus and species, not trade names), flagged as grown by you / bought from other nurseries / wild-collected. — NAPPRA taxa (Ficus, Dracaena, Hibiscus, Jasminum, Syzygium, bamboo, most palms) cannot appear on a US storefront at all; CITES taxa (all orchids, Nepenthes, Cycas, listed Euphorbia/Aloe) need a gated flow; every product needs a scientific-name field. Sansevieria/snake plant status under the Dracaena NAPPRA listing is unresolved — ask your quarantine office or APHIS ACIR directly before designing the catalog.
13. Are these common nursery plants ($15-40 pothos/Alocasia/Philodendron) or rare/variegated collector aroids and Nepenthes ($200-2,000)? — Changes whether a $100+ per-consignment phytosanitary + DHL cost is survivable, the fraud/chargeback profile, ad-policy exposure, and whether buyers already hold PPQ 587 permits (collectors often do; casual buyers never do).
14. Which model for the US importer: (a) each customer holds their own PPQ 587 (and PPQ 621 for CITES plants), (b) a US-resident entity holds the permits and re-ships domestically, or (c) a third-party US importer/consolidator? Name the entity. If you plan to use a service like Kartel Daun's "we are importer of record", what US entity is behind it? — Only US residents can hold PPQ 587. Model (a) needs a mandatory permit-number field, a "no permit, no shipment" gate and a shipping-address override to the Plant Inspection Station; (b)/(c) need a two-leg shipping model and a US entity in the legal pages.
15. Are you registered as an exporter with Badan Karantina Indonesia (SSM QC / PTK Online account), is your nursery registered as a place of production, which quarantine office and departure airport will you use, and does that office require the US buyer's import permit number before issuing the phytosanitary certificate — even for 12 or fewer plants? — Two Indonesian sellers report it does (unconfirmed on an official page); if true, the "12 or fewer plants, no permit" exemption is dead for Indonesian origin and the site must collect a permit before fulfillment, not after payment.
16. Will you sell any CITES-listed plants? If yes: do you hold SATS-LN/CITES export permits from Ditjen KSDAE, is your nursery registered as an artificial-propagation unit, and are any species on Indonesia's protected list (Permen LHK P.106/2018: 73 Nepenthes, Paphiopedilum, Phalaenopsis, etc.)? — CITES plants need a CITES export permit per shipment, a US Protected Plant Permit (PPQ 621) and entry via a designated port; selling protected wild plants is a crime in Indonesia and gets Meta/Google ad accounts suspended.
17. Exactly how will plants be packed: bare-root in damp sphagnum only, epiphytes on coconut husk/fiber or new clay pots/wooden baskets, or anything in soil/potting mix? Will roots be washed and treated before inspection? — Soil and growing media are prohibited (narrow epiphyte/tissue-culture exceptions); product pages must say "ships bare-root, no pot, no soil" and photos must not imply potted delivery.
18. Confirm every plant is under 2 years old and within size limits (roughly under 18 in for tree/shrub-habit plants incl. cycads, under 36 in for palm-like plants, under 4 in stem diameter / 6 ft height). — No XL/specimen variants for the US market. (Size table not read directly from the current Plants for Planting Manual; treat as indicative.)
19. Which US Plant Inspection Station/port will you route to (DHL LA, DHL Miami, FedEx Newark/Linden, SFO), which courier and service, has DHL/FedEx Indonesia approved your account for live plants to the US, and do you have the PPQ 508 label workflow (buyer obtains labels via APHIS eFile, emails them in color, seller attaches)? — Permit shipments must be addressed to the station on the permit; after clearance the package continues via USPS/the courier, so domestic-leg postage/waybill may need to be inside the box. Drives rates, tracking and address-override logic.
20. What is your per-box maximum, do you consolidate multiple customers' orders into one consignment, and will consignments be 12-or-fewer or 13-or-more plants? — 13+ must go to an inspection station with a mandatory permit; consolidation makes one importer responsible for everyone's plants. Sets cart quantity limits and order-splitting.
21. What are the per-order costs and lead times: BARANTIN inspection/PNBP fee, CITES fee if any, packing, DHL rate by weight/zone, US courier brokerage, domestic re-ship? Will they be built into prices or charged as a documentation surcharge? Realistic pre-shipment processing (sellers report 7-14 working days)? — Phytosanitary certificate is per consignment, so single-plant orders carry a large fixed cost; sets minimum order value and "ships within N days" copy.
22. What is your dead-on-arrival policy (report window, photo evidence, replacement vs refund), do you add heat packs, will you pause US shipping in winter (tropicals die below ~40°F over a 4-10 day window), and who absorbs the loss if USDA refuses or destroys a shipment? — Policy pages, seasonal shipping-hold switch, heat-pack add-on, time-boxed claim form, risk disclosure in terms.
23. Will you ship to Hawaii, Puerto Rico, Guam, and are you aware of state-level plant rules (CA/CDFA, FL, HI, AZ, TX nursery-stock and quarantine rules; all Myrtaceae are NAPPRA to Hawaii)? — Shipping-zone exclusions and address validation. State rules were not researched; flag for follow-up.

### Furniture — US import compliance

24. For every SKU, list each wood/rattan/bamboo component with scientific name (genus and species, e.g. Tectona grandis, Calamus manan) and country of harvest; if species can vary by run, list every species that could be used. — Formal (wholesale/container) entries need a Lacey Act declaration per HTS line; "teak" alone is not accepted for solid wood or rattan. Needs per-component species/origin metafields.
25. Do any products contain sonokeling/rosewood (Dalbergia spp.), ramin (Gonystylus), or any other CITES-listed wood? If yes, will each shipment carry an Indonesian CITES export permit and will the US importer hold a USDA Protected Plant Permit (PPQ 621, $70, 2 years)? — Dalbergia is Appendix II with only a 10 kg-of-listed-wood-per-shipment finished-product exemption (furniture exceeds it); ramin has no exemption. These SKUs must be excluded from the US storefront or made permit-only.
26. Is the manufacturer/exporter SVLK-certified and ETPIK-registered? Send the SVLK certificate number, certifying body, expiry, and confirm a V-Legal Document is issued for every wood-furniture shipment. Are rattan-only items exported under a separate ETR registration? — Without V-Legal, wood furniture cannot clear Indonesian export customs; a company cannot hold both ETPIK and ETR, so ask which entity exports what. Blocks launch if missing.
27. Where does your teak come from (Perhutani/plantation region, supplier names, reclaimed), and can you show none is Myanmar-origin? — Myanma Timber Enterprise is under OFAC sanctions and Myanmar teak transits third countries; false origin is a Lacey violation. Sustainability/legal-teak claims on the site need documents.
28. Are pallets and crates ISPM 15 heat-treated and stamped with the IPPC mark (ID code, facility number, HT/MB)? Who is the treatment provider? Is the furniture itself fumigated and is a certificate issued? — One unmarked pallet gets the whole shipment refused, re-exported or destroyed at the importer's expense.
29. Do any products include bark-on, live-edge, root, driftwood, or natural rattan/bamboo poles with skin intact, or anything not machined smooth and finished on all sides? — Finished smooth furniture is generally not an APHIS regulated article, but natural rattan/wicker with skin or borer holes is routinely held and fumigated at US ports; such SKUs need special handling or exclusion.
30. For every cushion/pillow: cover fabric, filling, whether marketed strictly for outdoor use, and what labels are sewn in (law label with contents and Uniform Registration Number; CPSC "Complies with U.S. CPSC requirements for upholstered furniture flammability" label if usable indoors)? — Outdoor-only cushions are exempt from 16 CFR 1640; indoor-use ones need TB 117-2013 testing and labelling. Product pages need an explicit "outdoor use only" statement.
31. Do any products contain plywood, MDF or particleboard (table tops, drawer bottoms, seat bases)? If so, is the panel supplier TSCA Title VI certified and is the finished good labelled "TSCA Title VI compliant"? — Unlabelled composite-wood goods are non-compliant on arrival; solid teak/rattan is outside the rule.
32. How is "Made in Indonesia" physically marked on each piece (branded, stamped, permanent label) and where? — 19 CFR 134 marking requirement; origin claim on the site must match.
33. What finishes, stains, oils, glues and sealants are used, and can you supply Prop 65 warning text per SKU (wood dust, formaldehyde)? How many employees does the selling entity have (under-10 exemption)? — California sales need the warning on the product page or a "WARNING" link before checkout, in the 2025 short-form format naming a chemical.
34. Do you offer any written warranty (teak retailers commonly advertise 1-5 years)? Who carries US product-liability insurance? — Magnuson-Moss requires warranty text viewable before purchase; liability insurance determines who is sued when a chair collapses.
35. Are every product design and every product photo your own, with rights to use them? — Jepara/Cirebon factories routinely reproduce Western catalog designs and reuse other factories' photos; copied photos get Merchant Center suspended and DMCA takedowns, copied designs invite trade-dress claims.

### Duties, tariffs & who pays

36. Will US orders ship DDP (you prepay duties, MPF, brokerage) or DAP/DDU (customer pays the carrier at delivery)? Answer separately for consumer parcels and for wholesale/container orders. — Every parcel is dutiable since the de minimis suspension. DDP needs HS code + origin on every product, Shopify duties-at-checkout (0.85%/1.5% per-order fee, DHL Express labels only) or duty-inclusive prices; DAP needs prominent "duties charged on delivery" notices at product, cart, checkout and email.
37. Do you have a licensed US customs broker (name) and a continuous customs bond? Can they file Type 11 informal entries and formal entries for courier shipments? — Without a broker, DDP is not feasible. Postal shipments now need a bonded owner/purchaser or designated broker as filer, and plants (PGA) and furniture bearing Chapter 99 duties are excluded from the simplified postal process (compliance date Oct 22, 2026) — "mail it via Pos Indonesia" is not a compliant option.
38. For every SKU, the 10-digit US HTS code and country of origin, and whether any seat has upholstery fixed/attached to a wooden frame versus loose cushions. — Upholstered wooden seats (9401.61.40xx/.60xx) pay 25% Section 232 (30% from Jan 1, 2027; no Indonesia cap); non-upholstered teak/rattan/metal pieces pay 0% MFN + 10% Section 301 instead. May justify selling frames and cushions as separate SKUs. Teak upholstered seats in 9401.61.20xx are an unresolved classification edge case.
39. Are your prices landed-cost (all-in) or ex-works/FOB Indonesia with duties added at checkout? — Landed-cost means fixed prices that must be re-priced every time tariffs change; ex-works means a duty calculator and a separate "Duties & import fees" line.
40. Have you priced in MPF (0.3464%, min $33.58/max $651.50 formal; $2.69 informal), HMF (0.125%, ocean only), courier brokerage/disbursement fees, and the per-consignment phytosanitary cost for plants? — On a $150 parcel these fixed fees exceed the 10% duty; decides whether fees are absorbed, a checkout line, or a shipping surcharge.
41. Who is responsible for updating prices/HS data when tariffs change, and how fast? — The regime changed at least five times in 2025-26; Section 232 rises Jan 1, 2027; Commerce's Oct 1, 2026 report may add other wood furniture to Section 232 within weeks. Choose live duty calculation vs manual tables with a "prices include estimated duties as of [date]" notice.
42. Will plants and furniture ever ship in the same consignment? — Mixing forces the whole shipment into formal entry with PGA handling; bare-root plants (0602.90.40/.90) pay MFN 3.5-4.8% + 10% Section 301 while orchids (0602.90.20) and unrooted cuttings are 301-exempt; rattan baskets/planters (4601/4602.12) are 301-exempt but furniture is not. May need separate shipping profiles so a cart cannot mix them.

### Shipping & fulfillment model

43. Is the model (a) single items to US households shipped from Indonesia, (b) US-held inventory at a 3PL, or (c) wholesale/container and project orders? What is the minimum order in pieces, CBM or USD, and is any finished inventory in stock or is every order a production run? — Jepara exporters run one-container MOQs; a B2C cart from Indonesia only works with fixed rates the partner commits to or a quote model. US-held inventory creates immediate sales-tax nexus and turns the site into domestic fulfillment.
44. Which freight forwarder/NVOCC and PPJK (customs broker) do you use, from which port (Semarang, Surabaya, Tanjung Priok), and can you send their written door-to-door quote (origin charges, ocean freight, destination CFS/THC, US brokerage, residential last-mile with liftgate)? — Decides whether checkout can show a shipping price at all or must be request-a-quote/deposit. Do not rely on blog per-CBM figures (found to vary 2-5x); only a real quote counts.
45. What incoterm do you sell on (EXW, FOB, CIF, DAP, DDP) to consumer and to wholesale buyers? — Terms-of-sale page and who arranges insurance and the US leg.
46. For each SKU: in stock or made to order, and realistic total lead time to US doorstep (production + export prep + sea transit ~25-45 days port-to-port + clearance + last mile)? Do you take a deposit, and when is the balance due? — Made-to-order timelines of 16-22 weeks collide with the FTC Mail Order Rule (ship within 30 days unless a date is stated; delays need notice/consent; refund if the buyer does not agree). Card authorizations expire long before shipment, so 50/50 terms must become two separate payments or immediate full capture, with a stated ship window on every page.
47. Per-SKU packed dimensions, gross weight, CBM, cartons per item, knocked-down or assembled? — Shopify manual rates only work on weight/price; any freight-quote app needs dimensions, DIM weight and freight class; native carrier rates (UPS/FedEx/USPS/Canada Post/Australia Post) cannot quote Indonesia-origin freight.
48. Which US destinations (continental only, specific coasts, AK/HI/PR), and any addresses you cannot serve (apartments without freight access, PO boxes)? — Shipping zones, address validation, phone-number requirement for freight appointments.
49. Is marine cargo insurance included, for what value (e.g. 110% of invoice), who is the named insured, who files damage claims, what evidence (photos at delivery, note on the delivery receipt) and what window? — Default LTL carrier liability is typically only $1-2/lb for new goods (up to $25/lb only at the highest freight class), so a $1,500 set may be covered for a few hundred dollars. Insurance line item and a claim form with photo upload.
50. What is your operating calendar (Idul Fitri factory/quarantine closures, monsoon packing constraints) and who answers US customers, in what hours, language and channel (WhatsApp is not acceptable to a US furniture buyer)? Who handles chargebacks and damage claims within card-network deadlines? — Shipping-hold dates, support tooling and response-time promises.

### Returns, support & liability

51. Are returns accepted at all, within how many days, with what restocking fee, who pays return freight, are made-to-order/custom items and cushions final sale, and where do returns physically go (a US address or Indonesia)? — 82% of US shoppers weigh free returns, but returning heavy goods to Indonesia is uneconomic; established US teak retailers charge return freight and make custom items final. Needs a returns policy page (required by card networks and Google Merchant Center) and a US return address if any.
52. Who is merchant of record on the receipt, and who handles refunds and chargebacks? — Managed Markets (Global-e as merchant of record) is unavailable to Indonesian stores; dispute-evidence capture (tracking, signatures) depends on the gateway.
53. Will you do email and SMS marketing to US customers, collecting unchecked opt-in and honoring STOP/unsubscribe? — TCPA needs prior express written consent for marketing SMS; CAN-SPAM needs a physical postal address in every email (whose?) and per-email penalties.
54. Which physical US postal address goes in marketing emails, on the About/contact page, and as the returns address? — Required by CAN-SPAM and Google Merchant Center's misrepresentation policy; must not default to the developer's home.

### Product catalog data

55. A complete catalog spreadsheet: SKU, name, variants (size/color/finish/material), dimensions (cm and in), weight, boxed/crated dimensions, HS code, materials with species and origin per component, price, stock quantity, lead time, warranty. — Product data model, shipping profiles, duty estimates and the Merchant Center feed all depend on it.
56. For plants: scientific and common name, size actually shipped, bare-root vs epiphyte-mounted vs tissue culture, CITES/protected status, and a photo of the plant at the size shipped, not a potted showroom specimen. — Species metafields, admissibility flags, compliance copy per listing, honest imagery.
57. High-resolution photos per SKU (white background plus lifestyle, multiple angles) with usage rights, and confirmation you own them. — See Q35; determines whether photography must be shot or commissioned before launch.
58. Brand assets: vector logo, palette, fonts, brand name and tagline, and whether the name has been cleared against USPTO and DJKI and who will own the trademark. — Theme setup and avoiding a rename after launch.

### Accounts, domain & brand assets

59. Do you own the domain, which one (.com, .id, .co.id), who is the registrant and registrar, and can you give me Cloudflare DNS access rather than registrar credentials? — .co.id needs NIB/KTP; a US audience expects .com. Shopify does not support Cloudflare proxy/O2O in front of the storefront, so Cloudflare is DNS-only (grey cloud); proxied records break SSL provisioning.
60. Who will be the owner of each account — Shopify, Google Ads, Google Merchant Center, GA4/GTM, Meta Business Manager and ad account, PayPal/gateway, domain, Cloudflare — and under which email, phone, country and tax ID? — Google Ads and Meta ad-account currency/country are permanent; Indonesian-billed Google Ads adds 11% VAT and requires an NPWP/NIK. Owner mismatches lock the developer out later.
61. Will you write or approve English content: About, shipping policy (transit, who pays duties, damaged-plant handling), returns/refund policy, contact page with a mailing address, terms, privacy policy (GA4/Meta Pixel plus state privacy laws and a consent banner; Indonesia's UU PDP and PSE registration for an Indonesian entity are unresolved)? Is an Indonesian-language version needed? — Merchant Center requires discoverable return, shipping, contact and About content; Shopify policy pages auto-link in checkout.
62. Confirm no CITES-listed plant or protected timber will appear in ad catalogs. — Meta bans ads for products from threatened/endangered species "including plants"; Google Shopping bans endangered-species trade; one listing can suspend the whole ad account and Merchant Center.

## Key facts (verified) that back this up

**Plants**
- Most plants for planting need a PPQ 587 permit; an e-commerce buyer "is considered an importer"; "Only U.S. residents may apply." Permit-required parcels go to a Plant Inspection Station on the permit; mail/courier shipments need buyer-obtained PPQ 508 labels; after clearance the package continues via USPS/courier (the station does not forward it). — aphis.usda.gov/plant-imports/buy-plants-seeds-online (last modified 2026-04-23); plant-inspection-stations page (2026-08-24). Corrected: original claim that the station forwards the box was wrong.
- "12 or fewer plants, only if no specific restriction applies" needs no permit; phytosanitary certificate still required in all cases. — APHIS page above; CBP APHIS trade guide App. APH-A ver. 4.2 (2026-06-15). Practical caveat: Indonesian sellers report BARANTIN demands the buyer's permit number even for <12 plants — unverified on any official page.
- Phytosanitary certificate mandatory for live plants from Indonesia (7 CFR 319.37-6); no equivalent-documentation arrangement exists for Indonesia. — law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/7/319.37-6 (e-CFR viewed 2026-09-16). Corrected: "small lots of seed is the only exemption" was wrong (paragraph (c) exists) but does not apply to Indonesia.
