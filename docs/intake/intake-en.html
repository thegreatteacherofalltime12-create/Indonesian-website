Start here: the three answers that fix the build

  1. Which product lines go on the US site.

    The outdoor line (LD: aluminium and synthetic rattan) is the simplest to sell into the US — no wood-species declaration, no Section 232 tariff, outdoor cushions exempt from US flammability rules. Indoor rattan (CH, LI, TBI) is fine too but carries more buyer paperwork. Crafts and ceramics are a different site.

  2. Who the buyers are and how prices are shown.

    US businesses only (retailers, designers, project buyers) or also consumers; prices public, behind a buyer login, or quote-only. Business-only with quote or gated pricing is the standard exporter site and the fastest to launch.

  3. Who at the company owns this project.

    A named contact, the contracting entity (PT Lemongrass Archipelagocraft Ekspor?), and who will own the domain and accounts. Everything else can be collected from the export and sales team as we go.

Product data

  1. The original Excel price lists with photos, product names and colours.

    The PDFs have codes only and "TBA" for most colours; the spreadsheets become the catalog dataset directly.

  2. Per SKU: gross weight, carton or packed dimensions, and whether shipped knocked-down or assembled.

    Needed alongside the CBM and pieces-per-40HC you already list, for the container-load calculator and freight quotes.

  3. Wood and rattan species by scientific name wherever wood or rattan is used (mahogany, teak, bamboo, rattan cane), and confirmation that no sonokeling/rosewood is in the US range.

    US buyers' brokers must file a Lacey Act declaration with genus and species; putting it on the product page removes a buyer objection. Rosewood is CITES-listed and most US buyers refuse it.

Key facts (verified) that back this up

What US buyers face with these products

  • Aluminium-frame outdoor seating with synthetic rattan is metal furniture for US customs: main codes are MFN duty-free plus the 10% Section 301 tariff in force since 2026-07-24; the 25% Section 232 tariff applies only to upholstered wooden seats. Outdoor-only cushions are exempt from the 16 CFR 1640 flammability rule. — hts.usitc.gov (2026-09-16); USTR (2026-07-23/28); CBP CSMS (2025-10-10); cpsc.gov.
  • Natural rattan and bamboo seating and furniture (9401.52/.53/.59, 9403.82/.83/.89) require a Lacey Act declaration with genus and species per component on formal entries (Phase VII, 2024-12-01). — aphis.usda.gov/plant-imports/file-lacey-act-declaration (2026-01-06).
  • Sonokeling/rosewood (Dalbergia spp.) is CITES Appendix II with only a 10 kg-per-shipment finished-product exemption; commercial import needs a USDA permit. — CITES CoP20 documents; aphis.usda.gov/plant-imports/cites (2026-09-09).
  • Wood packaging without the ISPM-15 mark is refused, re-exported or destroyed at the importer's cost. — aphis.usda.gov/plant-imports/wood-packaging-material/import (2026-07-27).
  • Indonesia–US ocean transit: FCL 27–39 days, LCL 30–42 days (indicative). — freightos.com (Sept 2026).

Indonesian export side

  • A V-Legal document (SVLK) is required for wood-furniture export customs clearance; whether rattan-only products are carved out is unresolved. — mongabay.com (2020-05-27), timbertradeportal.com (2020-06-09). Not independently re-verified.
